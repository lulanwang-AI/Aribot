How SynaptiFlow could stand out
Integrated 2D/3D markups across modalities – While New Lantern offers separate viewer modes and others focus on specific findings, SynaptiFlow would provide a unified viewer for CT, MRI and PET/CT, allowing doctors to seamlessly toggle between 2D slices and 3D renderings and annotate in either space. One‑click lesion segmentation and volume calculations could help in treatment planning.
Generative reporting and data prompts – Building on the natural‑language capabilities emerging in AI, SynaptiFlow could use large language models to summarize key findings, auto‑draft structured reports, suggest relevant clinical guidelines and allow users to ask contextual questions (e.g., “What are similar cases in our database?”). Competitors such as Aidoc and RapidAI focus on detection and triage; adding generative insights would improve efficiency for radiologists and referring physicians.
Holistic patient‑centric search – Combining imaging data with EHR, laboratory results and previous imaging, SynaptiFlow would enable complex searches (e.g., “all patients over 50 with previous CT brain showing microbleeds”) and highlight longitudinal changes. This goes beyond typical PACS search capabilities and could facilitate research and quality improvement.
Customizable AI pipeline – SynaptiFlow could act as an orchestrator that allows hospitals to plug in third‑party AI models (e.g., modules from Aidoc, Annalise, Lunit) through a marketplace-like interface. Hospitals could select which models to use per modality and track performance, following frameworks like Aidoc’s BRIDGE

 for governance. This openness contrasts with vendor‑locked platforms.
Care‑team collaboration and data routing – While Viz.ai and RapidAI focus on stroke workflows, SynaptiFlow would generalize secure communication for many specialties. When AI flags a finding, the system could automatically route the case to the appropriate department (e.g., send a suspected liver lesion to hepatology) and schedule follow‑up tasks. The system could track acknowledgments and integrate with hospital messaging to ensure accountability.
Privacy and audit‑centric design – With HIPAA compliance as a core requirement, the platform would incorporate encryption, role‑based access, comprehensive audit trails and on‑premises or hybrid deployment options. A continuous‑monitoring component would track model performance and fairness, aligning with regulatory guidelines.
By combining robust AI‑assisted detection with advanced visualization, generative insights, flexible integration and strong compliance, SynaptiFlow can deliver a comprehensive hospital‑infrastructure tool that addresses unmet needs in the radiology workflow and stands out from existing options.
